**Title:** Six questions to ask before you book Business English training for your team

**Article text:**

If you are choosing Business English training for a team, ask six questions before you sign: who teaches, how the programme is designed, what material it uses, how progress is measured, what the contract commits you to, and what you can see first. A provider who answers all six in writing is a safe choice. One who dodges two or more is not.

I have trained teams in Berlin and online for over ten years. These are the questions that separate a programme that works from one that only fills a calendar.

## 1. Who will teach every session?

Ask for a name, qualifications and business experience. Then ask if the same person teaches every session. Some schools sell the course and hand the group to a freelancer. A good answer names one person and says what happens if they are ill.

## 2. How do you find out what my team needs?

A good provider runs a needs analysis first: a conversation with you, a level check, and a look at the English your people handle at work. Warning sign: the course book is chosen before anyone asks a question.

## 3. What material will you use?

The best practice material is the team's own: the email due on Friday, the update given every Monday. Ask to see a sample. If every client gets the same book, it is not tailored.

## 4. How will we know it worked?

Ask for objectives in writing before session one, and make them observable.

- Good: "Write a client email in ten minutes without a colleague checking it."
- Weak: "Improve fluency."

A CEFR level (A1 to C2) helps you group people. It is not an objective. Attendance lists do not show improvement either.

## 5. What does the contract commit me to?

Ask for the price per session, the minimum number of sessions, cancellation rules and VAT. Ask what happens when people miss a session, and whether training is online or on site. Vague answers now mean a vague invoice later.

## 6. What can I see before I decide?

A free first conversation, a written proposal or sample exercises. You should not sign before you know what the training looks like.
