## Draft pair: following up on a quote with no reply

Setting: the writer sent a price quote for office furniture ten days ago and has heard nothing.

### Bad email

**Subject:** Re: Re: quote

Hi,

I sent you the quote last week and you didn't answer. Are you still interested or not? I need to know, because I have other customers waiting.

Thanks
[Your Name]

### Good email

**Subject:** Quote 2087 for 40 desks: do you have any questions?

Dear Mr Hoffmann,

I am writing to follow up on the quote (no. 2087) for 40 height-adjustable desks that I sent you on Monday, 26 October. I realise you may not have had time to look at it yet.

The prices in the quote are guaranteed until Friday, 20 November. If you would like to order before then, just reply to this email and I will confirm the delivery date the same day. If something in the quote is unclear, or the budget has changed, I would be happy to adjust it or call you for ten minutes.

Thank you for your time, and I look forward to hearing from you.

Kind regards,
[Your Name]
Sales Team, [Company]

### What changed

The "Re: Re:" subject tells the reader nothing, so the new one names the quote, the product and
a question. The bad email is annoyed ("you didn't answer", "or not") and pressures the reader
with other customers; the good one assumes the reader is busy. It gives the quote number and
the date it was sent, so the reader can find it in seconds, adds a real reason to answer now
(the price deadline), and offers an easy next step: reply to order, ask a question, or take a
short call. It stays short and asks for one thing.

### Signs to list (same format as 1167's "signs" box)

- A subject line that is only "Re: quote", with no number or product
- Blame or pressure ("you didn't answer", "I have other customers waiting")
- No reference: no quote number, no date sent
- A yes/no demand with no easy way to answer
- No deadline or reason to reply now
