You want to ask a simple question, so you say: <em>Where you are going?</em> The other person understands you. But you can hear that something is off, and you do not know what.

It is the word order. One small change fixes it, and after that your questions come out right without a pause.

## Questions that come out right the first time

Picture a phone call to a language school, or a chat with a new colleague. You ask <em>What time does the course start?</em> and <em>Where can I park?</em> You do not stop to think. The order is automatic.

Getting there takes one pattern, one German habit to unlearn and one trick for polite questions.

## One pattern for most questions

Put the question word first. Then the helping verb, then the subject, then the main verb.

> Where are you going?<br>What do you want for dinner?<br>Why is she late?<br>How long have you lived in Berlin?

The helping verb is <em>are</em>, <em>do</em>, <em>is</em> or <em>have</em>. It stands before the subject. In <em>Where you are going?</em> it stands after the subject. That is statement order, and it is the mistake.

## The German habit to unlearn

German puts the verb straight after the question word: <em>Wo wohnst du?</em> Copy that word for word and you get <em>Where live you?</em>

English needs <em>do</em> here:

> Where do you live?<br>What do you think?<br>When does the film start?

When <em>do</em> or <em>does</em> is in the question, the main verb stays in its basic form. So it is <em>When does the film start?</em>, not <em>When does the film starts?</em>

One exception is useful to know. When the question word is the subject, there is no helping verb: <em>Who called you?</em>

## A check you can do in two seconds

Before you speak, look for the helping verb. Is it between the question word and the subject? If it is, the order is right. If you have no helping verb, add <em>do</em>, <em>does</em> or <em>did</em>.

Say it aloud three times in a row: <em>Where are you going? Where are you working? Where are you staying?</em> The pattern sticks when you hear it, not only when you read it.

## Polite questions switch back

Now the order changes again. In a polite question, the question sits inside a longer sentence. It goes back to statement order, with no <em>do</em>:

> Where is the station? &rarr; Could you tell me where the station is?<br>What time does the train leave? &rarr; Do you know what time the train leaves?
