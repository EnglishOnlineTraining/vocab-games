About this task in the exam

True/False/Not Given is one of the question types in the IELTS Academic Reading test (60 minutes, 40 questions, three long texts). True means the text says the same thing. False means the text says the opposite. Not Given means the text does not say either way — so do not use what you already know about the topic.

Exercise A

Read and decide

Read the text, then decide whether each statement agrees with it (True), contradicts it (False) or is not covered by it (Not Given).

Before you start — optional, not marked
This is just to get you thinking — you can leave it empty and it does not count towards your score.
Reading text

Paths nobody planned

A  Walk across almost any city park and you will see them: narrow lines of worn grass or bare earth where people have left the paved path to take a shortcut. Planners call these “desire paths”. They usually appear where an official path makes walkers go around a corner or the long way round, and they can form in a single season if enough people take the same route.

B  For a long time, many park managers treated desire paths as damage. They put up fences, planted hedges or added signs asking people to keep off the grass. These measures sometimes worked, but walkers often found a way around them, and a new path soon appeared a few metres away.

C  Today, a growing number of designers see the paths differently: as free information about how people actually move. Some have suggested leaving the lawns around a new building unpaved for its first few months, then laying paths along the routes people have worn into the grass. Supporters say this produces paths that people use instead of ignore.

D  The approach has limits, however. Desire paths show where people want to walk, not where it is safe to walk: a shortcut across a busy road is still dangerous. They also reflect only the people who walk there most often, so a route that suits fit, hurried commuters may not suit wheelchair users or parents with buggies.

Statements
1. Desire paths usually form where an official path makes people take a longer route.
2. A desire path takes several years to form.
3. Desire paths are more common in cities than in the countryside.
4. Fences and signs always stopped people from using desire paths.
5. Most park managers now encourage people to walk on the grass.
6. Supporters believe the paths that result from this approach are actually used.
7. A desire path may not suit everyone who needs to use it.
Exercise B

Small words, big difference

Each item gives a sentence from a text and a statement. Compare the small words — usually, all, only, most, may — and choose True, False or Not Given.

Text → statement
1. Text: “The bridge is usually closed in winter.”
Statement: The bridge is never closed in winter.
2. Text: “The museum is free on Sundays.”
Statement: The museum is free every day.
3. Text: “Scientists are still unsure whether the drug works.”
Statement: Scientists have proved that the drug works.
4. Text: “Most visitors arrive by train.”
Statement: More visitors come by train than by any other form of transport.
5. Text: “Only a few of the original buildings survive.”
Statement: Some of the original buildings are still standing.
6. Text: “The festival was first held in 1998.”
Statement: The festival has taken place every year since 1998.
7. Text: “Almost all the farmers in the region grow rice.”
Statement: Rice is the only crop grown in the region.
8. Text: “The new law applies to companies with more than 50 employees.”
Statement: A company with 20 employees must follow the new law.
