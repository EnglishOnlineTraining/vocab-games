Useful Phrases for Business Emails

A professional email usually has the same parts: an opening, the information or request, arrangements, and a closing. These 18 phrases cover the six situations that come up most at work. Each one is a complete, correct sentence you can copy and adapt. They are written for learners at about B1 to B2 level.

Opening lines (3)

Thank you for your email regarding…
Use it to reply. Name the subject after “regarding”, for example “regarding the delivery date”.
I am writing to inquire about…
Use it when you write first and want information. Formal; “inquire” and “enquire” are both correct.
I hope this email finds you well.
A polite, stock opener for a first or formal message. You can leave it out in a quick note to a colleague.

Giving information (3)

As requested, I am sending you…
Use it when you attach or send what someone asked for. Say what it is: “the contract”, “the updated schedule”.
I would like to inform you that…
Formal, for news that matters: a change, a decision or a result.
Just a quick note to let you know that…
Friendlier and shorter. Good for small updates to people you already work with.

Making a request (3)

Would it be possible to…?
A polite way to ask for something that may be difficult. Follow it with a verb: “Would it be possible to move the meeting?”
I was wondering if you could…
Softer and less direct than “Could you…?”. Good when you are asking for a favour.
Could you please provide me with…?
A clear, polite request for information or documents. Common in formal emails.

Confirming arrangements (3)

I would like to confirm our meeting on…
Add the day and time. It gives the other person a chance to correct a mistake.
We have arranged to meet at…
Use it to state a place or time that is already agreed.
Just a reminder that…
A short, friendly nudge about a deadline or an appointment.

Apologizing (3)

I apologize for any inconvenience caused.
Formal. Use it when a problem affected the other person. British spelling: “apologise”.
We are sorry for the delay in responding to your email.
Use it when you answer late. Brief and honest; you do not need to explain at length.
Please accept our apologies for any misunderstanding.
Use it when something was unclear. It does not say whose fault it was.

Closing (3)

I look forward to hearing from you soon.
A standard ending when you expect an answer.
Thank you for your time and consideration.
Formal. Common after an application or a proposal.
If you require any further information, please do not hesitate to contact me.
A formal ending that invites questions. Some readers find it stiff; “Please let me know if you have any questions” is simpler.
