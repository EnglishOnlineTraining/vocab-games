Exercise A

Reading – Formal vs. Informal

Read the two emails below, then answer the questions.

Email 1
From: j.martinez@globaltech.com To: s.weber@partnerco.de Subject: Follow-up on project timeline

Dear Ms Weber,

Thank you for taking the time to meet with us last Thursday. I am writing to follow up on the points we discussed regarding the revised project timeline.

As agreed, we will deliver the first draft of the report by 15 July. Should you require any further information in the meantime, please do not hesitate to contact me.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Kind regards,
Julia Martinez
Project Manager, GlobalTech Solutions

Email 2
From: tom.k@globaltech.com To: lisa.m@globaltech.com Subject: Quick question about the report

Hi Lisa,

Hope you're doing well! Just a quick one – do you have the latest sales figures? I need them for the report that's due on Friday.

Let me know if you need a hand with anything on your end.

Cheers,
Tom

Exercise D

Writing – Compose a Professional Email

Read the situation below, then write a formal email using the phrases you have learned.

Situation

You work for a marketing company. Your team was supposed to deliver a draft campaign proposal to a client, Henderson & Co., by last Monday – but it was not ready on time. The draft is now complete and you need to send it to your contact, Mr David Henderson (d.henderson@hendersonco.com).

In your email you should: apologise for the delay, explain briefly why it happened (you can invent a reason), attach the proposal (mention that it is attached), and offer to arrange a meeting to discuss it.

Your email
Remember: greeting, purpose, apology, explanation, attachment reference, offer to meet, professional sign-off. Aim for 80–120 words.
