Exercise A

My Generation?

Read the text carefully, then answer the comprehension questions using the dropdown menus.

Reading Text

Every generation likes to believe it is different from the one before. Baby boomers were accused of being too idealistic, Generation X of being too cynical, and millennials of being too sensitive. Now it is Generation Z's turn to be criticised — usually by the very generations who once faced exactly the same complaints.

Part of what makes a generation distinct is the subculture it creates for itself. In the 1970s, punks used ripped clothes and loud music to reject mainstream values. In the 1990s, grunge and hip hop offered teenagers new identities and new soundtracks. Today's subcultures are less about a single music genre and more about online communities — gamers, K-pop fans, or climate activists who may never meet in person yet feel a strong sense of belonging.

Technology explains much of the gap. Older generations grew up without smartphones and learned patience the hard way, whereas today's teenagers have never known a world without instant messaging. This does not necessarily make one generation better than another; it simply means each generation solves problems with the tools available to it. Older relatives often assume that less screen time automatically means a more meaningful life, but that comparison is not always fair.

What almost all generations have in common, however, is being underestimated by their elders. The music, fashion and slang of every youth subculture have, at some point, been dismissed as a passing phase — and yet each one has left its mark on culture. Perhaps the most useful skill any generation can learn is not to judge the next one too quickly.
