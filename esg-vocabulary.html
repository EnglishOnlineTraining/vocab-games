ESG stands for environmental, social and governance: the three areas investors and companies use to judge how responsibly a business is run. This glossary is for anyone who reads, writes or talks about sustainability at work in English, from sustainability reports to investor meetings. It covers the core ideas, ESG investing and finance, and company practice and reporting.

Core ideas (10)

carbon footprint
The amount of greenhouse gas that a person, product or company releases, for example through transport and energy use.
circular economy
An economic system that cuts waste and makes the most of resources by reusing and recycling materials.
climate change
The long-term shift in weather patterns: higher temperatures, more frequent extreme weather and rising sea levels.
diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI)
Respecting and valuing differences such as race, gender, age, ability and sexual orientation, and making sure everyone is treated fairly at work and in society.
environmental justice
Fair treatment and real involvement of all people, whatever their race, origin or income, in making and enforcing environmental laws and policies.
human rights
The basic rights and freedoms every person has, such as the right to life, liberty and security, and freedom from discrimination and torture.
renewable energy
Energy from sources that are naturally replaced, such as solar, wind and hydropower.
social responsibility
A company's duty to act fairly towards its stakeholders: employees, customers, communities and the environment.
sustainability
The ability to meet today's needs without harming the environment, society or the economy for future generations.
sustainable development goals (SDGs)
17 goals set by the United Nations to build a more sustainable future by 2030, including ending poverty, reducing inequality and fighting climate change.

Investing and finance (8)

climate risk
The possible financial and non-financial harm that climate change can cause to a company or an investment portfolio.
ESG investing
Investing that looks at a company's environmental, social and governance practices as well as its financial results.
governance risk
The possible harm caused by poor corporate governance, such as fraud, corruption and conflicts of interest.
green bonds
Bonds sold to pay for projects with environmental or climate benefits, such as renewable energy or energy-saving work.
impact investing
Investing in companies or projects that aim to create a measurable social or environmental benefit as well as a financial return.
socially responsible investing (SRI)
Investing only in companies that meet ethical or social standards, for example avoiding tobacco, alcohol or weapons.
stakeholder capitalism
A view of business that creates value for all stakeholders (employees, customers, communities and the environment), not just shareholders' profits.
sustainable investing
Putting money into companies with sustainable business practices and a good record on environmental, social and governance issues.

Company practice and reporting (12)

carbon disclosure
Reporting a company's carbon emissions and other environmental figures to investors, customers and regulators.
corporate governance
The rules and processes that make sure a company is run in an ethical, responsible way, including board oversight, executive pay and shareholder rights.
corporate social responsibility (CSR)
Voluntary actions a company takes to benefit society and the environment, often going beyond what the law requires.
environmental impact assessment (EIA)
A study of the likely effects of a project on the environment, done before the project is approved or started.
environmental management system (EMS)
A structured way of managing a company's effect on the environment, with policies, procedures and performance measures.
greenwashing
Making false or exaggerated claims about a product's environmental benefits to attract customers who care about the environment.
impact measurement and management
Assessing, tracking and reporting the social and environmental effects of a company's activities.
social entrepreneurship
Using business methods to create lasting social or environmental change.
social impact
The positive effects a company's activities have on society, such as creating jobs, developing communities and reducing poverty.
stakeholder engagement
Involving stakeholders such as customers, employees and local communities in a company's decisions.
supply chain sustainability
Building environmental, social and governance standards into a company's supply chain, including responsible sourcing of raw materials and fair treatment of workers.
water stewardship
Using and managing water responsibly, including protecting water quality and using less of it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does ESG stand for?
Environmental, social and governance. The three areas are used to judge how responsibly a company is run, and by investors who want to put money into responsible companies.

30 English terms in plain English, grouped by topic: core ideas such as sustainability and the circular economy, ESG investing and finance, and company practice and reporting.
