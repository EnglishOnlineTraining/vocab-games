Exercise A

Inside India – Reading

Read the text about Mumbai, Indian companies, and volunteering, then answer the comprehension questions.

Reading Text

India is the most populous country in the world, with over 1.4 billion people. Its largest city, Mumbai, is home to more than 20 million residents and serves as the country's financial capital. Mumbai's skyline is a mix of modern glass towers and historic colonial buildings. The city is famous for Bollywood, India's enormous film industry, which produces over a thousand films every year — more than any other film industry in the world.

India's economy has grown rapidly in recent decades, driven largely by the technology sector. Companies such as Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) and Infosys employ hundreds of thousands of people and provide IT services to businesses around the globe. However, the issue of fair wages remains a serious concern. Entry-level engineers at major IT firms often earn starting salaries that have barely increased in over ten years, even though the cost of living has risen significantly. Many young workers feel that the gap between executive pay and the wages of ordinary employees is too large.

At the same time, India has a growing culture of volunteering, especially among young people. Organisations such as Teach For India recruit volunteers to work in schools in disadvantaged communities, helping children with reading, writing, and mathematics. In Mumbai alone, thousands of volunteers give up their weekends to teach in community centres, organise health camps, and run environmental clean-up projects. Many Indian schools now include community service as part of the curriculum, encouraging students to understand the challenges faced by less fortunate members of society.

Fair trade is another important movement in India. Fair trade organisations work with small-scale farmers and craft workers, ensuring they receive a reasonable price for their products. Tea, coffee, spices, and textiles are among the most common fair trade goods exported from India. By buying fair trade products, consumers in Europe and elsewhere can help support better working conditions and fair pay for Indian producers.

If-Clauses Type I & II

Complete the sentences about India with the correct if-clause form. Remember: Type I (if + simple present → will + infinitive) describes real/possible situations. Type II (if + simple past → would + infinitive) describes unreal/hypothetical situations.

Grammar reminder

Type I (real/possible): If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home.

Type II (unreal/hypothetical): If I lived in Mumbai, I would visit Bollywood studios every week.
