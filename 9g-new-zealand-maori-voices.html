Exercise A

Aotearoa: Language, Land and Identity

Read the text, then answer the comprehension questions.

Text

New Zealand is also known by its Māori name, Aotearoa, often translated as “land of the long white cloud”. Māori people arrived in the islands centuries before European settlement and developed rich traditions connected to family, land, navigation and storytelling.

Te reo Māori, the Māori language, is an official language of New Zealand. For a long time, its use declined because English dominated schools and public life. In recent decades, however, language nests, bilingual schools, television programmes and public campaigns have helped more young people learn and hear te reo Māori.

Māori culture is visible in many parts of national life. A marae is a meeting place where ceremonies, speeches and shared meals connect people to their ancestors. The haka, a powerful performance with movement and voice, can express welcome, pride, grief or challenge, depending on the occasion.

Environmental ideas are also important. Kaitiakitanga means guardianship: the responsibility to care for land, water and living things. For many New Zealanders, protecting the landscape is not just about tourism; it is about respect for a place that carries memory and identity.

Comprehension Questions
1. What does Aotearoa often mean in English?
2. What is te reo Māori?
3. Why did the use of te reo Māori decline?
4. What is a marae?
5. What does kaitiakitanga refer to?
