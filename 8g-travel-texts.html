Text 1: Travel Blog – "Big Sky Country"

Day 3 on the road and I am completely hooked on Montana. They call it "Big Sky Country" and now I understand why — the sky here feels enormous, stretching from horizon to horizon over wide-open plains and jagged mountain peaks. Yesterday we drove for four hours and barely passed another car. The silence was extraordinary.

We spent the morning hiking in Glacier National Park. The trails are well-marked and the park rangers were incredibly helpful — one of them told us that the park has over 700 miles of trails but only about 25 glaciers left, down from 150 a century ago. Climate change is having a real impact here. In the afternoon, we stopped at a tiny diner in a town of about 300 people. The locals were so friendly — a retired cowboy named Jim showed us photos of the valley from fifty years ago and said it looks completely different now.

Tomorrow we are heading to Missoula, which has a surprisingly lively arts scene for a city of its size. A friend recommended a bookshop there that sells nothing but used books and has a cat sleeping in every corner. I cannot wait.

Text 2: Travel Guide Extract – Montana for Adventure Seekers

Montana is one of the last truly wild places in the contiguous United States. With its vast national forests, crystal-clear rivers and majestic mountain ranges, it offers some of the best outdoor experiences on the continent. Whether you are an experienced hiker, a keen fly-fisherman, or simply someone who wants to escape the noise of the city, Montana has something for everyone.

Getting around: A car is essential. Public transport is extremely limited outside the main cities of Billings, Missoula and Great Falls. Distances between towns can be vast — plan your fuel stops carefully and always carry emergency supplies.

Best time to visit: Summer (June–August) offers the best weather for outdoor activities, but also attracts the most visitors. For a quieter experience, consider visiting in early September, when the crowds have thinned and the autumn colours are beginning to appear.

Top tip: Montana has strict rules about campfires during dry periods to prevent wildfires. Always check local fire regulations before lighting a fire, and pack a portable stove as a backup.

Exercise D

Writing – Your Travel Blog

Write a short travel blog entry (80–120 words) about a real or imaginary trip. Use collocations from Exercise B, adjectives from Exercise C, and at least one descriptive detail that makes your writing vivid.

Choose one idea

Idea 1 — A day trip: Write about a day trip you have taken (or would like to take). Describe where you went, what you saw and how you felt. Use travel collocations like set off, take in, come across, head to.

Idea 2 — Your dream destination: Imagine your perfect travel destination. Describe what makes it special — the landscape, the people, the food or the activities. Use vivid adjectives like stunning, majestic, vast, remote.

Idea 3 — A travel tip: Write a short travel guide entry for your hometown or region. What should visitors see and do? What practical advice would you give? Use the travel guide style from the reading texts.
