# Frameworks as post skeletons

Read only the section for the framework you chose. Each section gives the
beats, how they map to the post, an example skeleton and the traps.

Contents: 1 Before-After-Bridge · 2 Challenge-Choice-Outcome · 3 Hero's
Journey · 4 3-Act Structure · 5 And-But-Therefore · 6 Pixar Story Spine

---

## 1. Before-After-Bridge

**Beats**
- **Before:** the reader's current pain, in their words, with one specific detail.
- **After:** what life looks like when the problem is gone. Make the reader want it. Do not explain how yet.
- **Bridge:** the method. This is the longest part and holds the example, practice task and CTA.

**Post map:** opening (Before, 1–2 paragraphs) → short After section → Bridge as 2–4 how-to sections → CTA.

**Example skeleton** (learners, topic: writing English emails faster)
- Opening: "You have written the first line of the email three times. It is still not right."
- H2 "Ten minutes, not forty" (After)
- H2 "Start with the reason, not the greeting" (Bridge step 1)
- H2 "Five phrases that do most of the work" (Bridge step 2, example)
- H2 "Try it with your next email" (practice + CTA)

**Traps:** a vague Before ("emails are hard"); explaining the method before showing the After.

## 2. Challenge-Choice-Outcome

**Beats**
- **Challenge:** a real dilemma with two or more reasonable options. Show why it is hard.
- **Choice:** what was chosen and the reasoning. This is the core.
- **Outcome:** an honest result, including what did not work, and the lesson for the reader.

**Post map:** opening dilemma → the options → the decision and why → what happened → what the reader can take from it (example, task, CTA).

**Example skeleton** (teachers, topic: correcting every error in student writing)
- Opening: "Thirty Year 9 essays. Do you correct every mistake, or only some?"
- H2 "Two options, both with a cost"
- H2 "Why I chose to mark only three error types"
- H2 "What changed, and what did not"
- H2 "Try it with your next set of essays" (task + CTA)

**Traps:** a dilemma with an obvious answer; an outcome that is only success. Use "I" only for Shaun's real decisions; otherwise use a typical scenario.

## 3. Hero's Journey (5 beats)

Twelve stages are too many for a blog post. Use five.

**Beats**
- **Ordinary world:** where the hero starts.
- **Call:** what changes or challenges them.
- **Mentor:** what helps them - a method, a habit, a resource.
- **Ordeal:** the hardest moment.
- **Return with the elixir:** what the hero can now do, and what the reader can take.

**The hero** is the reader ("you") or a clearly typical learner. The site or method is the mentor, never the hero.

**Post map:** one section per beat; the elixir section holds the example, task and CTA.

**Example skeleton** (learners, topic: first meeting in English at work)
- Opening: "For three years, your job was in German. Then the company hired a team in Dublin."
- H2 "The first meeting invitation"
- H2 "One habit that changes everything"
- H2 "When the client asks a question you do not understand"
- H2 "What you can say now" (phrases, task, CTA)

**Traps:** making the business the hero; skipping the Ordeal; ending on success instead of on what the reader gains.

## 4. 3-Act Structure

**Beats**
- **Act 1, Setup:** who, where, what problem. Stakes are clear by the end.
- **Act 2, Conflict:** the first attempts, the obstacles, the fix that fails. The longest part.
- **Act 3, Resolution:** the breakthrough, the result and the lesson.

**Post map:** Setup (1 section) → Conflict (2 sections) → Resolution (1–2 sections with example, task, CTA).

**Example skeleton** (HR/L&D, topic: an engineering team that avoids English calls)
- Opening: "The team writes excellent English emails. On calls, nobody speaks."
- H2 "More vocabulary did not help"
- H2 "The real problem was the first ten seconds"
- H2 "What worked: three opening phrases, practised every week"
- H2 "Try it with your team" (task + CTA)

**Traps:** a two-line Act 2 - without struggle, the resolution means nothing.

## 5. And-But-Therefore

**Beats**
- **And:** the situation - what is normal or expected.
- **But:** the turn - what goes wrong or changes. Make it sharp.
- **Therefore:** what follows - the solution, the action, the lesson.

Use ABT at two levels: as the spine of the whole post, and inside paragraphs to keep tension. Do not bold or label "and", "but", "therefore".

**Post map:** short And (1 paragraph) → But (1 section) → Therefore (the rest: explanation, example, task, CTA).

**Example skeleton** (learners, topic: present perfect vs past simple)
- Opening: "You know both tenses, and you can explain the rule."
- H2 "In real conversation, the rule disappears"
- H2 "One question that decides the tense: is the time finished?"
- H2 "Five sentences to test yourself" (task + CTA)

**Traps:** a weak "but"; more than one "but" in the spine.

## 6. Pixar Story Spine

**Beats**
Once upon a time… Every day… Until one day… Because of that… Because of that… Until finally… And ever since then…

The second "Because of that" is where the first fix fails and things get harder. Keep it.

**Post map:** the spine works best as a narrative opening (3–6 short paragraphs), then sections that unpack "Until finally" (the method) and "ever since" (the new normal, example, task, CTA). Never use the spine's phrases as headings or sentence openers in sequence.

**Example skeleton** (parents, German, topic: Vokabeln lernen)
- Opening: "Ihr Kind lernt jeden Abend Vokabeln. Am Montag im Test sind sie weg."
- H2 "Mehr lernen hat es schlimmer gemacht"
- H2 "Zehn Minuten, drei Tage, laut sprechen"
- H2 "So sieht es heute aus" (Beispiel, Aufgabe, CTA)

**Traps:** writing "Once upon a time" literally; skipping the failed first fix.
