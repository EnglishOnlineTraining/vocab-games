Exercise A

Listening to Indigenous Voices in Canada

Read the text, then answer the comprehension questions.

Text

Canada is home to many Indigenous peoples, including First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities. These groups have distinct languages, traditions and histories, and they lived on the land long before Canada became a country. Today, Indigenous cultures are visible in art, music, literature, politics and environmental activism.

Canada’s history also includes serious injustice. For many years, Indigenous children were forced to attend residential schools, where they were often separated from their families, languages and cultures. Survivors and their families have asked the country to listen honestly to this history and to work toward reconciliation.

One sign of change is language revitalization. In some communities, elders teach young people words, stories and songs that were almost lost. Schools and museums increasingly include Indigenous perspectives instead of presenting history from only one point of view.

Many public events now begin with a land acknowledgement, a short statement that names the Indigenous peoples connected to the territory. Acknowledgements are not enough on their own, but they can remind listeners that history is connected to real places and real communities.

Comprehension Questions
1. Which three groups are named in the text?
2. What happened in residential schools?
3. What is language revitalization?
4. What is a land acknowledgement?
5. What does the text suggest about reconciliation?
