This glossary is for people who work with offices, shops, warehouses and other commercial property in English: agents, property managers, investors, lenders and the tenants and landlords they deal with. It covers lease types and rent, loans and finance, returns and valuation, the legal side of buying, and the running of a building. Leases and rent (15)

CAM (common area maintenance) charges
Fees tenants pay towards looking after shared areas such as parking lots and lobbies. In the UK: service charge.
commercial lease
A legal agreement that lets a tenant use a commercial property in return for rent.
commercial lease agreement
The written, legally binding contract between landlord and tenant that sets out the terms of a commercial lease.
concessions
Incentives a landlord offers to win a tenant, such as a rent-free period or a discount.
escalation clause
A lease term that raises the rent over time, often to keep up with inflation. In the UK: rent review.
gross lease
A lease where the rent includes the building's running costs, so the landlord pays items such as property tax and insurance.
ground lease
A lease of land only. The tenant rents the land and owns, or builds, the building on it.
letter of intent (LOI)
A short document that sets out the main terms of a proposed lease or sale. It is usually not legally binding. In the UK: heads of terms.
market rent
The usual rent for similar commercial space in the same area.
net lease
A lease where the tenant pays some of the property's operating costs on top of the rent.
rent roll
A list of all the tenants in a property with their lease terms and rent payments.
sublease
A lease where the tenant rents all or part of the space to another tenant, while still holding the original lease.
triple net lease (NNN)
A net lease where the tenant pays the property taxes, insurance and maintenance as well as the rent. Common in the US.
undermarket rent
Rent that is lower than the current market rent. Usually written below-market rent.
work letter
A document in a lease that sets out what building work is needed and who does and pays for it.

People and management (12)

absentee landlord
An owner who does not live on or near the property and relies on a property manager to run it.
asset management
Managing a portfolio of properties as investments, to make them as valuable and profitable as possible.
brokerage
A business whose agents (brokers) arrange the buying, selling or leasing of property for a fee. The fee itself is also called brokerage.
franchise
A business arrangement where one company lets another use its name and business model in return for a fee and a share of the profits.
homeowner's association (HOA)
A body that runs a residential community, looks after shared areas and enforces its rules (the CC&Rs). Mainly American.
landlord
The owner of a property who rents it to a tenant. Contracts may say lessor; the tenant is the lessee.
lease administration
Managing leases day to day: collecting rent, dealing with tenants and handling renewals.
leasing agent
A licensed professional who helps landlords find tenants and negotiates lease terms.
operating agreement
The legal document that sets out how a company, usually an LLC, is run and how its investors share profits. Property held by several investors is often owned through such a company.
property management agreement
A contract between an owner and a property manager that sets out the manager's duties and pay.
property manager
A person or company that runs a property for its owner.
tenant screening
Checking a possible tenant's finances and rental history before signing a lease.

Loans and finance (17)

amortization
Paying off a loan gradually through regular payments that cover both interest and part of the principal.
balloon payment
A large final payment due at the end of a loan. The smaller monthly payments before it do not pay off the whole loan.
debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
A property's net operating income divided by its loan payments. A ratio above 1 means the income covers the debt.
equity
The value of a property minus the debt owed on it.
equity capital
Money investors put into a property deal in return for ownership or a share of the profits.
hard money loan
A short-term, high-interest loan from a private lender, based on the value of the property rather than the borrower's credit.
liquidity
How quickly something can be turned into cash. Property has low liquidity.
loan-to-value ratio (LTV)
The loan amount divided by the value of the property, as a percentage.
mezzanine financing
Financing that ranks below the main (senior) loan and above the owner's equity, and so carries a higher interest rate.
mortgage
A loan to buy property, secured on that property. If the borrower defaults, the lender can take the property.
pre-approval
A lender's early check of a buyer's finances to say how much they could borrow.
principal
The amount borrowed that is still to be paid back, not counting interest.
recourse loan
A loan where the borrower is personally liable. If the property does not cover the debt, the lender can claim other assets.
seller financing
A deal where the seller lends the buyer money to buy the property.
subordination agreement
An agreement that puts one debt below another, so that the other lender is paid first.
underwriting
The lender's assessment of the risk of a loan before deciding whether to approve it.
variable rate loan
A loan whose interest rate goes up and down with a benchmark rate.

Value and returns (18)

absorption
The rate at which available commercial space is leased or bought over a period of time.
appraisal
An expert's estimate of a property's value, for example for a mortgage or insurance.
assessed value
The value a local government gives a property to calculate its tax.
asset
Anything of value that an investor owns.
capital expenditures (CapEx)
Large one-off spending to buy or improve a property, such as a new roof or heating system.
capitalization rate (cap rate)
A property's net operating income divided by its value or price, as a percentage. It shows the return from income alone.
cash-on-cash return
The yearly cash income from a property as a percentage of the cash the investor put in.
depreciation
The fall in a property's value over time because of age, wear and tear or becoming out of date. For tax, it is also the cost written off each year.
fair market value
The price a property would sell for on the open market between a willing buyer and a willing seller.
investment property
A property bought to earn money from rent or from selling it later.
operating expenses
The regular costs of running a property, such as property taxes, insurance and maintenance.
passive income
Income from a property that needs little day-to-day work from the owner.
pro forma
A forecast of a property's income and costs, used to estimate how profitable it could be.
real estate investment trust (REIT)
A company that owns and runs income-producing property and pays most of its profits to shareholders.
sale-leaseback
A deal where an owner sells a property and rents it back from the buyer.
vacancy
Space in a building that is empty and not being used.
vacancy rate
The share of space in a building or market that is empty, as a percentage.
yield
The yearly return on a property as a percentage of its price. Used in the UK and Europe where Americans often say cap rate.

Buying, legal and compliance (16)

building code
Rules for the design, construction and upkeep of buildings in an area.
certificate of occupancy (CO)
A document that confirms a building meets local building codes and may be used.
covenants, conditions and restrictions (CC&Rs)
Rules that control how property in a community or development can be used.
deed
A legal document that transfers ownership of a property.
due diligence
The checks a buyer or tenant makes before signing: inspections, legal review and financial analysis.
easement
The right to use part of someone else's land for a set purpose, such as access or utility lines.
encroachment
When a building or structure extends beyond its owner's boundary onto neighbouring land.
escrow
An arrangement where a neutral third party, the escrow agent, holds money or documents until all the conditions of a sale are met.
inspection
A professional check of a property's condition, usually before a sale.
insurance
Cover that protects a property against loss from damage, liability claims and other risks.
land use restriction
A legal limit on how land can be used, such as zoning or environmental rules.
lien
A legal claim on a property for an unpaid debt, such as a tax bill or a mortgage.
offer
A proposal to buy a property, with the price and terms.
property tax
A tax paid to local government on land and buildings, based on the assessed value.
title insurance
Insurance that protects an owner or lender against losses from problems with the property's legal title.
zoning
Local government rules that decide what a piece of land may be used for.

Building and operations (14)

building automation system (BAS)
A system that monitors and controls a building's heating, cooling, lighting and other equipment to save energy and cost.
building class
A grading of commercial buildings, often A, B or C, based on age, location and facilities.
emergency preparedness plan
A plan for how to respond to emergencies such as fires or natural disasters.
energy management
Using a building's energy as efficiently as possible to cut costs and improve sustainability.
ESG (environmental, social and governance)
Standards for judging a company's sustainability and ethics, including how it manages its properties.
fire safety systems
Equipment that prevents or controls fires, such as sprinklers and fire alarms.
HVAC system
Heating, ventilation and air conditioning: the system that controls a building's temperature and air.
improvement
Any change that adds value to a property, such as a renovation or an extension.
leasehold improvements
Changes made to rented space, usually by or for the tenant, that become part of the building.
maintenance and repair
The ongoing work needed to keep a property in good condition, such as painting, cleaning and plumbing repairs.
security
Measures that protect a property from theft, vandalism and other threats.
tenant improvement (TI) allowance
Money a landlord gives a tenant in the lease to pay for changes to the rented space.
tenant improvements (TI)
Changes made to a rented space to suit a particular tenant. The landlord often builds them or pays for them.
utilities
Services a building needs to operate: electricity, water and gas.
