Exercise A

Same Same But Different? – Reading

Read the text about cultural diversity in modern societies. Then answer the comprehension questions.

Reading Text

Walk through any major European city today and you will hear dozens of languages, see restaurants from every corner of the globe, and meet people whose families came from five different continents. Cultural diversity — the presence of many different cultures, ethnicities and perspectives within one society — has become one of the defining features of the modern world. But is it always a source of strength, or can it also be a cause of tension?

Supporters of multiculturalism argue that diverse societies are more creative and innovative. Research has shown that teams made up of people with different backgrounds tend to come up with more original solutions to problems because they bring a wider range of experiences and viewpoints. Cities like London, New York and Berlin are often cited as examples of places where cultural mixing has produced vibrant art, music and food scenes. Immigration has also been an economic benefit in many countries, helping to fill shortages of skilled workers in fields such as healthcare, engineering and technology.

Critics, however, point out that diversity can create challenges. When people from very different backgrounds share the same public spaces, schools and workplaces, misunderstandings and conflicts can arise. Some argue that without a shared sense of values or identity, social cohesion — the feeling that members of a society belong together — is difficult to maintain. Integration takes time and effort from all sides: newcomers must learn the language and customs of their new home, while the host society must be prepared to accept change and challenge its own assumptions.

Most sociologists agree that the key is not to force people to abandon their cultural identity, but to find common ground. A successful diverse society does not require everyone to be the same — it requires mutual respect, equal rights, and opportunities for all. As one researcher put it: "The goal is not a melting pot, where differences disappear, but a mosaic, where each piece keeps its colour while contributing to a larger, more beautiful picture."

Exercise D

Writing – Diverse Societies

Write a comment or short essay (100–150 words) on one of the following topics. Use linking words from Exercise B and vocabulary from Exercise C.

Choose one topic

Topic 1 — Mosaic or melting pot? Do you think diverse societies are stronger when people keep their cultural identities, or when they adapt to a shared culture? Defend your position using arguments from the text and your own ideas.

Topic 2 — Diversity in your community: Is your own city or region culturally diverse? What examples of cultural mixing do you see around you? Do you think this diversity is a strength or a challenge — or both?

Topic 3 — "Same same but different": Think about a time you noticed that different cultures approach the same situation in very different ways (e.g. greeting customs, food, school culture). What did you learn from that experience?
