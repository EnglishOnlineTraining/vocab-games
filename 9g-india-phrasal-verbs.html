Exercise A

Reading – India: A Land of Many Colours

Read the text carefully, then answer the questions below.

Text

India is the seventh-largest country in the world by area and the most populated country on Earth, with over 1.4 billion people. It is located in South Asia and is bordered by Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and Myanmar. The country stretches from the snow-capped Himalayas in the north to tropical beaches in the south.

One of the most remarkable things about India is its incredible diversity. More than 20 languages are officially recognised, and hundreds more are spoken across the country. Hindi and English are the two most widely used languages for government and business. India is home to many religions, including Hinduism, Islam, Sikhism, Buddhism and Christianity, and people of different faiths often live side by side.

Indian culture is famous around the world for its colourful festivals. Diwali, the Festival of Lights, is celebrated in October or November. Families put on their best clothes, light oil lamps and set off fireworks to mark the victory of light over darkness. Holi, the Festival of Colours, takes place in spring. During Holi, people throw coloured powder and water at each other and dance in the streets.

India also has a rich tradition of storytelling, music and film. The Indian film industry, known as Bollywood, produces more than 1,500 films every year – more than Hollywood. Cricket is by far the most popular sport, and millions of fans follow the national team closely.

India gained independence from British rule on 15 August 1947, after a long struggle led by Mahatma Gandhi. Gandhi believed in non-violent protest and never gave up his fight for freedom. Today, India is the world’s largest democracy and one of the fastest-growing economies on the planet.

Comprehension Questions
1. How many people live in India?
2. Which two languages are most widely used for government and business in India?
3. What does the festival of Diwali celebrate?
4. How does India’s film industry compare to Hollywood?
5. How did Mahatma Gandhi fight for India’s independence?

Grammar Reminder

A phrasal verb is a verb combined with a preposition or adverb (or both) that creates a new meaning. For example, give up does not mean the same as give + up separately – it means “to stop doing something”.

Example: Gandhi never gave up his fight for freedom.
