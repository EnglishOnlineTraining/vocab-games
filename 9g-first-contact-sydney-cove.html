Exercise A

Watch the film and take notes

Your teacher shows the film in class. Keep this page open and write your notes while you watch.

Before you watch — optional, not marked
This is just to get you thinking — you can leave it empty and it does not count towards your score.
Background to the film

The film is a historical re-enactment. In January 1788 the first British ships sail into Sydney Cove. Captain Arthur Phillip sets up a British penal colony there, which later grows into the city of Sydney.

King George III has told Phillip to build friendly relationships with the Aboriginal people who live there. But they would rather keep away from the newcomers. In the end, Phillip has one of them kidnapped: a man called Woollarawarre Bennelong.

Task a

Take notes about what happens after Bennelong has been kidnapped by the British soldiers. Sort your notes under the three headings.

Short phrases are fine. Write one point per line.
Exercise B

“Both men were terribly wrong.”

The narrator says this about the future relationship between Aboriginal and white people.

Task b
Use your notes from Exercise A, especially “Hopes for the future”. Aim for about 80–120 words.
Exercise C

Can you trust a re-enactment?

The film shows real events from 1788 with actors, costumes and a narrator.

Task c
Weigh up the pros and cons, then give your opinion with at least two reasons and one example from the film. Aim for about 120–150 words.

Compare with a model answer

Read it and tick off what you also had. Your answer can be different and still be good.

a) Captain Phillip and Bennelong

  • Bennelong is forced to learn the English language and English manners.
  • Phillip and Bennelong become friends (e.g. Christmas dinner).
  • Bennelong runs away. Aboriginal custom says he must take revenge on Phillip.
  • Bennelong attacks Phillip, but Phillip survives and forgives him.
  • ‘Coming in’: Bennelong apologises to Phillip.
  • Bennelong moves into ‘Bennelong Point’.

Hopes for the future

  • White people: Aboriginal people will easily become part of colonial society and culture.
  • Aboriginal people: they hope to keep their traditions, peace and independence.

What happens later on

  • White settlers drive Aboriginal people off their land and kill them.
  • Thousands die from European diseases.
  • 100 years after the British arrive, 80% of the Aboriginal population has died.

b) Phillip and Bennelong both thought that white people and Aboriginal people could live together peacefully. The future showed that both were wrong: thousands of Aboriginal people were killed, and they lost their land and their culture.

c) Pros: fun to watch; informative; they make history interesting; it is easier to imagine what life was like; the audience identifies with the characters.

c) Cons: they need expensive research; a re-enactment is not an original source and does not show the events exactly as they happened; it appeals to the emotions and tells a story, so is it really true?
