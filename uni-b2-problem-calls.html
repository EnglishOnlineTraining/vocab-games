Exercise A

Diplomatic phone language

On the phone you cannot edit your words like in an email. These phrases help you stay polite and still get what you need. Choose the best option for each gap.

Exercise B

Three calls

Work in pairs in your breakout room. Each call is harder than the one before. Choose your role, read only your own card, and switch cameras on if you can. You have a goal and a secret: do not read your partner's card and do not show yours. After each call, write down what you agreed.

Language bank

Asking politely

  • I'm calling about…
  • Could you possibly…?
  • Would it be possible to…?
  • I'd really appreciate it if you could…

Saying no politely

  • I'm afraid that won't be possible.
  • Unfortunately, I can't… but I can…
  • I see your point, but…
  • That's not something I can decide alone.

Pushing back

  • I understand that, but…
  • Is there really no way to…?
  • With respect, that doesn't solve the problem.
  • What would you do in my position?

Calming things down

  • I completely understand why you're upset.
  • I'm sorry this has happened.
  • Let's see what we can do.
  • Here's what I can offer…

Offering a deal

  • What if we…?
  • How about a compromise: …
  • I could…, if you could…
  • Would that work for you?

Checking and closing

  • Sorry, I didn't quite catch that.
  • So, if I understand you correctly, …
  • Just to confirm: …
  • I'll send you an email to confirm.
Call 1: The heating (about 8 minutes)

Warm-up. Person A calls Person B. In a group of three, the third person is the Observer.

A: Tenant in Flat 4

  • The heating has been broken since Monday. You have already emailed twice.
  • You work from home and the flat is 15°C.
  • Your goal: a repair by tomorrow evening.
  • You can only be at home on Thursday morning.

🔒 Secret: if the repair has to be later, you would accept something warm for now, but do not offer this yourself. Let your landlord suggest it.

B: Landlord

  • Your normal technician is fully booked until next Monday.
  • You can bring an electric heater to the flat this afternoon.
  • Your goal: keep the tenant happy without paying extra.

🔒 Secret: an emergency service could come tomorrow, but it costs €150 extra. Only offer it if the tenant stays polite and gives you good reasons. Try to agree that the tenant pays half.

Observer

  • Do not speak during the call. Listen for phrases from the language bank.
  • Write down two good phrases you heard and one moment where someone sounded too direct.
  • After the call, tell your partners. Then write it below.
Call 2: The deadline (about 12 minutes)

Swap: last time's B is now A. This time the answer at first is no. Do not accept it too quickly.

A: Student

  • Your essay is due this Friday. You were ill for four days last week, and you also work 20 hours a week.
  • Your doctor's note covers only two of the four days.
  • Your goal: one more week.

🔒 Secret: you can accept three extra days, but no less. About half of your essay is already finished.

B: Lecturer

  • The department rule: no extension without a doctor's note for the full time.
  • You have already given five extensions this term and the marking is late.
  • Your goal: be fair to the other students. Say no at first.

🔒 Secret: you can give up to 72 hours if the student shows a clear plan, for example by sending you the finished half of the essay today. You cannot give a full week.

Observer

  • Listen for how A pushes back and how B says no.
  • Write down one phrase that pushed back well and one refusal that sounded polite.
  • Did they reach a deal? Was it fair for both sides?
Call 3: The angry caller (about 12 minutes)

Swap again. The caller starts angry. The officer must stay calm and polite and must not promise what they cannot do.

A: Student (angry)

  • Last night the online system crashed while you were registering for your exam. Today the exam office wrote: “The deadline has passed.”
  • Your scholarship needs this exam this semester.
  • Your goal: get registered.

🔒 Secret: start angry, but stay polite (no insults). Calm down only when the officer shows they understand you and offers something concrete. You took a screenshot of the error message.

B: Exam office

  • The deadline was clear and was in three emails.
  • Your goal: calm the caller down and find a fair solution, without breaking the rules.

🔒 Secret: you know the system was down for two hours on the last evening. You can register a student late only with a short written statement and proof (e.g. a screenshot) sent by 12:00 tomorrow. Do not promise that it will work for sure: your manager decides.

Observer

  • When did the caller start to calm down? What did the officer say just before?
  • Write down one phrase that calmed things down.
  • Did the officer promise anything they should not?
Exercise C

Confirm the deal

After a call, a short email makes the agreement clear. Choose one of your three calls and write the follow-up email from your role, on your own, in about 7 minutes. Do not use translation software.

Word count: 0 (aim for 50–80)

Thank the person, say exactly what you agreed (who, what, when) and close politely.
