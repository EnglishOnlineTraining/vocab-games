Exercise A

Reading: Two Countries, Countless Journeys

Read the text carefully, then answer the questions below. Some questions ask about content, others ask you to interpret the statistics yourself.

Before you read — optional, not marked
This is just to get you thinking — you can leave it empty and it does not count towards your score.
Two Countries, Countless Journeys

Migration has reshaped both the United Kingdom and the United States over the past few decades, though the reasons people give for moving differ sharply between the two countries. In the UK, work and family reunification dominate: net migration to the UK has risen sharply since 2000, climbing from around 160,000 people a year to a peak of over 600,000 in 2022, before falling back to roughly 340,000 the following year.

Surveys of new arrivals suggest that just under half name employment as their main reason for coming, while a further quarter arrive to study at British universities. The remaining group — made up largely of refugees and family members joining relatives already settled — accounts for the rest.

Across the Atlantic, the pattern looks rather different. Immigration to the United States has grown far more gradually: the number of new lawful permanent residents has remained relatively stable at around one million a year for over a decade, dipping only briefly during the pandemic before recovering. According to the most recent Population Survey, family ties bring in the majority of new arrivals, while employment-based visas account for a smaller, though still significant, share.

What both countries share is a growing public debate about how to manage these numbers fairly — a debate that shows no sign of settling any time soon.

Comprehension & interpreting the data

1 How does UK net migration change over the period described in paragraph 1?

2 According to the surveys mentioned, roughly what share of new arrivals to the UK say employment is their main reason for coming?

3 Besides work and study, what does the text say makes up the remaining group of UK arrivals?

4 How has immigration to the United States changed over the past decade, according to paragraph 3?

5 According to the Population Survey mentioned in the text, what is the most common reason for new arrivals to the US?

6 What do both countries have in common, according to the final paragraph?

Exercise D

Writing: Infographic Captions

Your school’s Global Studies magazine is publishing an infographic about migration, and the editor has asked you to write the captions. You have been given these figures about work-related migration to New Zealand:

Your data
  • 2010: 5,900 people migrated to New Zealand for work
  • 2018: 14,200 people migrated to New Zealand for work
  • 2023: 13,100 people migrated to New Zealand for work
  • Of all migrants arriving in 2023: 61% came for work, 24% for family reunification and 15% for study

Write two sentences describing the trend in work-related migration from 2010 to 2023, and one sentence describing a proportion from the 2023 breakdown — as if they were the captions under the infographic. Use at least one trend phrase (e.g. has risen, has fallen, peaked) and one proportion phrase (e.g. the majority of, just under a quarter). Aim for 40–70 words.
