Exercise A

Australia in Danger

Read the text. Choose the correct word for each gap.

Before you read — optional, not marked
This is just to get you thinking — you can leave it empty and it does not count towards your score.
Reading text

Australia's nature is famous all over the world, but it is in danger.

Australia is a very dry country, and long periods without rain, called droughts, are common. When the land is hot and dry, bushfires start easily and spread fast. In the "Black Summer" of 2019–2020, huge fires burned across the south-east. Scientists think nearly three billion animals were killed or lost their 1.

The Great Barrier Reef is more than 2,300 kilometres long, and it is built by tiny sea animals called coral. When the ocean gets too 2, the coral turns white and can die.

Some dangers came from other countries. In 1859, a farmer 3 24 rabbits into the wild. Today there are millions of rabbits, and they eat the 4 that native animals need. In 1935, cane toads were brought from Hawaii to eat beetles on sugar cane 5. The toads didn't eat the beetles, but they spread across the north. Their skin is 6, so snakes and lizards die when they eat them.

Animals and plants like these, which come from other places and cause problems, are called invasive species.

Exercise D

Writing: Save Australia's Nature

Your partner school in Brisbane is making a website called Save Australia's Nature for visitors. Write 6–8 sentences for it: explain one danger from the text and tell visitors what they should and shouldn't do.

Plan your text

• Which danger do you choose: drought, bushfires, the reef or invasive species?

• Why is it a problem for animals and plants?

• What should visitors do? What shouldn't they do?

You can use these: One big danger is ... This is a problem because ... Visitors should ... Visitors shouldn't ... If you ..., you will ...
Your text
Aim for 6–8 sentences (at least 30 words).
