📘 Referenzleitfaden: Zusammenfassungen schreiben (zum Nachschlagen)
Present tense — summaries of written texts are written in the simple present, even if the article uses the past.
Example: "The article explains that..." (not "explained")
Reporting verbs — use verbs like states, argues, suggests, claims, points out instead of "says".
Example: "The writer argues that ambition has changed shape."
Objectivity — summaries report the writer's ideas, not your own opinion.
Example: Avoid "I think..." or "In my opinion..."
Umbrella sentences — group several specific details under one general statement.
Example: Instead of listing three examples, write: "The article gives several examples of this trend."
Conciseness — cut examples, repetition, and rhetorical flourishes; keep only the core content.

The Ambition Gap: Why Young Americans Are Rewriting the Rules of Success

Adapted from a US current-affairs magazine feature

For most of the twentieth century, the American Dream had a simple shape: work hard, climb the ladder, and the good life would follow. Ask any twenty-five-year-old today whether that promise still holds, and you will likely be met with a tired laugh. Something has shifted, and it is not merely a matter of economics.

Consider Daniel Ruiz, a 27-year-old marketing associate in Ohio. He grew up watching his father clock sixty-hour weeks at a steel plant that closed twice and reopened once, chasing a stability that never quite arrived. Daniel now works four days a week by choice, earning less than his father did, and describes himself as happier than his parents ever were.

He is far from alone. A recent Pew survey found that 61% of American workers under thirty now rank "time for life outside work" above salary when choosing a job. Employers who once dangled titles and corner offices are discovering that a new generation simply is not biting.

Critics call this laziness. Sociologists call it something else entirely: a rational response to a broken bargain. Their grandparents built companies; their parents built portfolios; they are building, above all, boundaries. The ambition has not disappeared — it has relocated, from the office to the self.

Whether this shift represents decline or wisdom will be argued for years. But one thing is already clear: the old ladder is rusting, and nobody under thirty seems particularly interested in climbing it.

Selbsteinschätzung

Model summary:

The article discusses how young Americans are redefining ambition and success. It explains that, unlike previous generations who prioritised career advancement and financial stability, many workers under thirty now value free time and personal boundaries above salary and status. The writer illustrates this shift through the example of Daniel Ruiz, who deliberately works fewer hours than his father did and reports being happier as a result. The article also cites survey data showing that a majority of young American workers rank work-life balance above pay. It concludes that this change should not be seen as laziness, but rather as a rational adaptation to a career system that younger generations no longer trust.
