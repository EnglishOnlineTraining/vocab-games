Exercise A

Leaving and Remembering Ireland

Read the text, then answer the comprehension questions.

Text

Ireland has a long history of emigration. During the Great Famine in the 1840s, potato crops failed and many people faced hunger, poverty and disease. More than a million people died, and many others left for Britain, North America or Australia, hoping to build safer lives.

The Irish diaspora stayed connected to Ireland through letters, music, religion, family stories and later through newspapers and community organisations. In cities such as New York, Boston and Liverpool, Irish emigrants formed neighbourhoods where they could support each other and keep traditions alive.

Emigration did not end in the nineteenth century. In the twentieth century, young Irish people continued to leave when there were few jobs at home. Later, during the economic boom known as the Celtic Tiger, some returned, while others arrived from different countries and changed Ireland into a more multicultural society.

Storytelling remains central to Irish identity. Songs, novels and films often explore leaving home, missing family and imagining a return. These stories show that migration is not only a statistic; it is also an emotional experience that shapes memory, language and belonging.

Comprehension Questions
1. What caused many people to leave Ireland in the 1840s?
2. Where did many Irish emigrants go?
3. How did the diaspora stay connected to Ireland?
4. What was the Celtic Tiger?
5. What does the text say about migration stories?
