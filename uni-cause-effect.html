Climate Change and Food Security

Read the text and complete the gaps with the correct cause-effect connector.

📖 Reading Text

Climate change is one of the greatest challenges facing global agriculture today. Rising temperatures and shifting rainfall patterns are disrupting crop production worldwide. , many farmers in tropical regions are experiencing lower yields and shorter growing seasons.

Water scarcity is a particularly serious concern. prolonged droughts and unpredictable monsoons, irrigation systems can no longer keep pace with demand. This has had devastating effects on smallholder farmers, who often lack the resources to adapt.

Higher temperatures also encourage the spread of agricultural pests and diseases. Insects that were once confined to tropical zones are now moving into temperate areas warmer winters allow them to survive year-round. , crop losses from pest damage have increased by an estimated 10–25% in several regions.

The nutritional quality of crops is also at risk. Elevated levels of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere can boost plant growth; , research shows that this often reduces the protein and mineral content of staple grains such as wheat and rice.

Addressing these challenges requires coordinated global action. governments invest in climate-resilient farming practices and support vulnerable communities, food insecurity is likely to worsen in the coming decades.
