Exercise A

The Outback: More Than an Empty Space

Read the text, then answer the comprehension questions.

Text

When people imagine Australia, they often think of beaches and modern cities. However, much of the continent is made up of remote inland regions known as the Outback. These areas are dry, thinly populated and sometimes hundreds of kilometres from the nearest hospital, supermarket or school.

Life in the Outback requires planning and cooperation. The Royal Flying Doctor Service, founded in 1928, uses aircraft to bring medical help to people who live far from towns. Some children learn through online lessons or radio contact because daily travel to school would be impossible.

The Outback is also culturally important. Uluru, the huge red rock in the Northern Territory, is sacred to the Anangu people, who have lived in the region for thousands of years. Visitors are asked to respect local rules and learn why the site matters instead of treating it as only a photo opportunity.

Tourism brings money to remote communities, but it can also create problems. Travellers who drive long distances must carry water, check road conditions and plan fuel stops carefully. During droughts or extreme heat, small mistakes can become dangerous. Responsible tourism means respecting both the environment and the people who call the Outback home.

Comprehension Questions
1. What is the Outback?
2. Why do some children in the Outback learn online or by radio?
3. Why is Uluru important to the Anangu people?
4. What should travellers do before driving long distances?
5. What is the main message of the text?
