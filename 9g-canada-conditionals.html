Canada – A Land of Extremes

Canada is the second-largest country in the world by area, stretching nearly 10 million square kilometres from the Atlantic Ocean in the east to the Pacific Ocean in the west. To the north lies the Arctic Ocean, making Canada the only country that borders three oceans. Despite its enormous size, Canada has a relatively small population of around 40 million people. Most Canadians live within 200 kilometres of the border with the United States, where the climate is milder.

The country’s geography is incredibly varied. The Rocky Mountains run through western Canada, with peaks reaching over 3,900 metres. The central provinces of Manitoba, Saskatchewan, and Alberta contain vast prairies — flat grasslands that are ideal for farming wheat and canola. In the east, the Great Lakes form part of the border with the United States and hold about 20 percent of the world’s fresh surface water.

Canada’s climate can be extreme. Winter temperatures in the northern territories regularly drop below minus 30 degrees Celsius, and some communities are so remote that they can only be reached by plane or by ice roads that form in winter. Summers in southern Canada, however, can be warm and humid, with temperatures above 30 degrees.

Indigenous peoples have lived in what is now Canada for thousands of years. There are three main groups: First Nations, Inuit, and Métis. The Inuit traditionally live in the Arctic north and have developed remarkable skills for surviving in one of the harshest environments on Earth. First Nations peoples are found across the country and have diverse cultures, languages, and traditions. Today, there are over 70 distinct Indigenous languages still spoken in Canada, though many are in danger of disappearing.

Canada is officially bilingual, with both English and French as national languages. The province of Quebec is predominantly French-speaking, while the rest of the country mainly speaks English. This bilingual tradition goes back to the 18th century, when both Britain and France had colonies in what is now Canada.

Remember: Conditional Sentences

Type 1 (First Conditional) — real/possible situations in the future:

If + present simple, … will + infinitive

If you visit Canada in winter, you will need warm clothes.

Type 2 (Second Conditional) — unreal/imaginary situations:

If + past simple, … would + infinitive

If I lived in Canada, I would learn to ski.

Fill the gaps
